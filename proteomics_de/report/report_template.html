Framing spine · both tracks

The experiment, and its hard ceiling

A SILAC comparison in mouse: testosterone-treated material against vehicle control. Two runs per condition — technical replicates of one biological sample, not a population.

The four measured channels are 31579, 31581 (vehicle control) and 31578, 31580 (testosterone-treated). Because both replicates re-measure the same biological material, their p-values describe “is this ratio reproducible on this instrument,” not “does testosterone change this protein in the population.” That distinction is the governing constraint of the entire analysis, and it is why the significance line is never reached.

A correction worth flagging. The original design document assumed a human system (taxid 9606). Inspection of the committed data showed otherwise: 99.7% of gene symbols are title-case MGI nomenclature (Lama1, Sptan1, Cryab), and H2-K1 is a mouse MHC class-I gene. The organism was corrected to Mus musculus, taxid 10090, and every organism-specific step — STRING, g:Profiler, Enrichr libraries — was re-pointed to mouse. Human parameters would have returned meaningless mappings.

A second correction, and a bigger one: the treatment assignment was inverted. This pipeline originally read 31578, 31580 as control and 31579, 31581 as treated, inherited from the design document. The lab's own earlier Pilot Project says the opposite — its cleaned tables and cleaning script name the columns Vehicle_Rep1_31579, Vehicle_Rep2_31581, Testosterone_Rep1_31578, Testosterone_Rep2_31580. The check that settled it: on the 30 proteins common to the pilot's cleaned table and this pipeline's output, the two log₂ fold changes correlate at r = −0.82 with sign agreement of 2/30. Mirror images. The pipeline was flipped to match the lab.

Every direction in this report is therefore the corrected one, and the numbers below are not the ones an earlier draft carried: 509 up / 206 down is the reverse of the 206/509 that shipped before. Three things are worth stating plainly. The replicate pairing never changed — rep 1 is 31578/31579, rep 2 is 31580/31581, as it always was; only the direction inverts. Every p-value is unchanged, because swapping two group labels negates log₂FC and t but leaves |t| and therefore p exactly invariant — the headline null is untouched by this correction. And the fix itself was a one-line edit to config/sample_sheet.tsv, which is precisely the payoff of having made that sheet load-bearing.

A note on the isotope labels, since SILAC invites the assumption: the workbook's Protein Report L and Protein Report H sheets record which sheet a channel's row lives in, not which condition it belongs to. All four samples are complete SILAC runs carrying their own Light, Heavy and H/L columns, and this pipeline analyses the combined Intensity — it never uses the native H/L ratio. Whether that is the right quantity is an open question filed for the lab, not a settled one.

Track A — Method

Three ways to average a ratio wrong

The legacy 88-line script produced UP / DOWN calls — with three correctness bugs baked into the fold-change logic. The first one changes the answer, not just the rounding.

Bug 1 — arithmetic mean of ratios, then log. SILAC ratios are multiplicative; their correct centre is the geometric mean (equivalently, the mean of the log₂ ratios). Averaging the raw ratios first systematically inflates apparent up-regulation. The worked example below is the whole bug in two numbers.

Worked example — one protein, two runsBug 1 · arithmetic vs geometric mean
Run 1 · H/L ratio
2.0×
twofold up in run 1
Run 2 · H/L ratio
0.5×
twofold down in run 2
Legacy · arithmetic mean → log₂
(2.0 + 0.5) / 2 = 1.25
log₂(1.25) = +0.32  → reads “UP” · WRONG
Correct · geometric mean = mean of log₂
√(2.0 × 0.5) = 1.00
log₂(1.00) = 0.00  → no change · CORRECT

Bug 2 — asymmetric thresholds on the log₂ scale. “UP” fired at ratio ≥ 1.5 (log₂ = +0.585) while “DOWN” required ratio ≤ 0.5 (log₂ = −1.0). A protein had to rise only 1.5× to be called up, but fall a full 2× to be called down. The symmetric partner of a 1.5× cut is 1/1.5 = 0.667 (log₂ = −0.585) — so the classifier was quietly biased against detecting down-regulation.

Bug 3 — inf / NaN propagation. Ratios were computed for every row before the zero/missing filter, so a zero control-channel denominator produced inf, and log₂(0) produced −inf. These non-finite values were excluded from the UP/DOWN call but survived in the saved artifact, ready to break any downstream consumer that did not re-filter.

And a silent fourth. The inner join on accession kept only proteins seen in both sheets — discarding the on/off proteins that are often the strongest biological signal, with no log. Corrected in the next section.

The three arithmetic bugs are fixed at the source: fold changes are the mean of log2_rep1 and log2_rep2, the regulation cutoff is symmetric at |log₂FC| ≥ 0.585, and non-finite values never reach the saved table. Just as important, the merge no longer silently loses data: the 604 single-condition and 10 on/off proteins the inner join used to drop are now carried into their own audited CSVs as separate candidate pools (returned to in Track B). Of the 1,948 both-condition proteins, 715 clear the |log₂FC| ≥ 0.585 effect-size gate — 509 up, 206 down — but that is an effect-size list, not yet a significance claim. That comes next.

The single-condition pool reads 604, not the 606 an earlier draft carried. Two rows of the raw Light sheet held a semicolon-joined list of bare MaxQuant row indices — 32,759 and 681 characters long — where an accession belongs, and the schema used to carve an explicit exception so they would pass validation. They are now quarantined to their own file instead. The test discriminates on token shape (every token a bare integer), never on length: two other long accessions of 69 characters, such as P08752;P20612;…, are legitimate protein groups and are retained. Nothing else moved — those rows are single-condition, so they never had a fold change, never entered limma and never reached the IPA export; only the enrichment background shrinks, 2,554 → 2,552.

Quality control that surfaces bad news

Good QC is not a green checklist — it is the part of the pipeline allowed to disagree with you. Two checks return WARN, and the report keeps them in view rather than tuning them away.

The honest limitation, surfaced. Raw intensities reproduce well between runs (Pearson r = 0.84 control, 0.86 treated). But the fold-change — a difference of two large, similar numbers — is noisy: replicate r = 0.27, below the 0.50 gate, so the check returns WARN. Subtracting two near-equal quantities amplifies small wobbles, so per-protein up/down calls at n = 2 are not individually trustworthy. This is not a failure to hide; it is precisely the on-paper justification for limma, which borrows information across all ~1,938 proteins to stabilise each estimate when single-protein replicates cannot.

The centering check likewise returns WARN (mean log₂FC = +0.28 against a ±0.10 tolerance) and writes a centered copy for inspection, without overriding the “no normalization” rule that the recipe calls for. Per-sample intensity distributions are near-identical (medians 27.4–27.8 log₂), which is the basis for not adding a cross-sample normalization step; and most “missingness” is structural — proteins genuinely absent from one condition — rather than random dropout.

One loose end, stated rather than hidden: the standalone qc_replicate_correlation.csv still labels those two raw-r values with the pre-correction assignment, because replicate_check.py hardcodes its channel pairs instead of reading the sample sheet and so was not carried along by the treatment-assignment flip. The values are right and the pairing is right; only the two column names in that one QC file are swapped. The figures on this page, which do read the sheet, are correct — and the numbers quoted above are the corrected ones.

limma + MinProb, and the line nothing reaches

The statistically defensible core, per Peng et al. 2024: no extra normalization, MinProb imputation for left-censored missingness, and a limma moderated t-test with empirical-Bayes shrinkage.

With two values per group, a per-protein t-test has essentially no power and a wildly unstable variance estimate. limma’s empirical Bayes shrinks each protein’s variance toward a global trend — the one thing that makes inference possible at small n. It runs cleanly here, and the result is unambiguous: 0 of 1,938 proteins reach FDR < 0.05. The default fit is the field-standard eBayes(trend = TRUE, robust = TRUE), which the recipe specifies and which handles the intensity-dependent variance and the outlying proteins that a two-replicate design produces; its smallest adjusted p-value is 0.116, with 55 proteins at a nominal raw p < 0.05. Plain vanilla eBayes is kept alongside it as a byte-reproducible comparison and lands at 0.305 with 63 nominal — a visibly more conservative tail, and the same verdict. Neither flavour puts anything across the line, which is the point: the conclusion does not depend on that choice.

Critically, corr(limma log₂FC, pipeline log₂FC) = 1.0000 across the 1,578 proteins where both estimates are fully observed: the contrast is wired correctly, so the null is a real property of the data at n = 2, not a plumbing error. The volcano therefore draws the raw p = 0.05 line for orientation only — there is no FDR line to draw, because nothing crosses it. Each row of the limma output now also carries n_imputed, the count of values MinProb invented for that protein; since MinProb is stochastic at n = 2, a reader can tell a measured estimate from a partly-manufactured one instead of having to trust that they are the same thing.

Track A — Method

The background that manufactures a story

The most instructive result in the whole report is a trap avoided. Choose the wrong enrichment background and a null dataset produces a confident pathway narrative.

The background-inflation trap

Over-representation asks whether your gene list is enriched relative to a background. The right background is the detected proteome — the 2,552 proteins that could have been seen. The naive background is the whole genome.

0

522

(326 up + 196 down; both topped by GO:CC “cytoplasm”, p ≈ 9.0 × 10⁻⁷⁰ and 1.9 × 10⁻²³)

Same two gene lists, same tool, same corrected-p threshold — only the background changes. That whole-genome story is an artifact of comparing detected proteins to every gene in the mouse, rather than to what this instrument could have detected at all; “cytoplasm” tops both directions because a detected proteome is cytoplasm-rich by construction, not because testosterone did anything. The pipeline uses the detected-proteome background and therefore does not fall into it — the honest answer at n = 2 is that no enrichment survives correction.

Both enrichment tests agree with the per-protein null and are reported as separate statistical tests from it — never conflated. g:Profiler ORA against the custom background returns 0 terms in either direction (best corrected p ≈ 0.70). Preranked GSEA over 568 mouse gene sets returns 0 at FDR < 0.05 (minimum FDR q = 0.127). The sub-threshold leads that do exist are carried into Track B, labelled for exactly what they are.

Track A — Method

Analyses that know when to switch off

Every multivariate method registers a minimum sample count. A dispatcher runs only those whose requirement is met and logs the rest — so PCA and clustering appear here strictly as QC, not as biology.

At n = 4 the sample covariance matrix is rank-deficient: exactly three principal components exist by construction, eigenvalues are biased, and PC1 can be driven by a single sample. So PCA and hierarchical clustering run in QC-only mode — a sanity check that technical replicate pairs sit near each other — below the trustworthy gate of n ≥ 6. Heavier methods are skipped outright, each with its reason recorded in a machine-readable registry.

Track A — Method

Reproducible by construction

A single declarative config.yaml is the source of truth for design, thresholds and file contracts; adversarial schema validation checks the frozen outputs against it.

Five committed CSV contracts are validated with pandera schemas built by inspecting the real data, not an idealised spec — they accommodate O/P/Q-prefixed UniProt accessions, semicolon-joined protein groups, NaN gene names, and one narrowly-scoped documented data quirk, while still rejecting anything genuinely malformed. The last run passed 5 / 5 files, with cross-file arithmetic checked too: 1,948 − 10 = 1,938 and 1,948 + 604 = 2,552. MinProb is stochastic, so its seed is fixed (42); package versions are pinned; and outputs are frozen — the reproducibility layer never edits the pipeline it describes.

Themes worth a second experiment

The strongest sub-threshold signals from ORA and GSEA. Every one of these is not significant — listed as directions to probe, never as findings.

On the DOWN side, the best (still non-significant) ORA lead is dephosphorylation (GO:BP, corrected p ≈ 0.70, 11 genes including Ppp2ca, Ppp1cc, Ppp1ca, Ppp5c and Ppm1a), alongside mRNA-splicing Reactome terms that share the Sm-like genes Lsm2 and Lsm6. These term lists changed direction with the treatment-assignment correction, as expected. Both readings were non-significant, so no conclusion moves; but the current one is the one that can be checked.

Two candidate pools the old join threw away

The proteins that are present in one arm and absent in the other are often the most interesting — and were exactly what the legacy inner join discarded without a log.

On / off · 10 proteins

All-or-nothing signals

Detected in one condition and genuinely zero in the other — the most dramatic candidate switches. Too few, and too extreme, to test with limma (a whole condition would have to be imputed), so they are kept as a labelled pool rather than forced through a model.

Single-condition · 604 proteins

Present in only one arm

Identified in control-only or treated-only, with no cross-condition ratio to compute. Excluded from limma by design — imputing an entire missing condition manufactures data — but retained as a rich, separate follow-up pool once biological replicates make their presence/absence pattern testable.

Together these two pools account for the gap between the 1,938 tested and the 2,552 detected. Nothing is deleted silently; everything is filed where it can be picked up again — including the two quarantined junk accessions, which are written to their own file rather than dropped on the floor.

Forward path · both tracks

What unlocks with biological replicates

The ceiling here is replication, not architecture. Growing the study starts with a config change — but it is not only a config change, and the honest version of that claim is worth more than the tidy one.

Adding biological replicates starts by appending rows to sample_sheet.tsv (new sample IDs, replicate index 3, 4, …). From there the pipeline splits cleanly into two halves, and it is worth being precise about which is which.

Genuinely automatic. The statistical core re-derives itself from the sheet with no code change at all: etl/build_matrix.py regenerates the intensity-matrix and design contracts, and limma_test.R --design builds model.matrix(~group) from whatever that file says — the design matrix really does regenerate. viz/style.py derives its sample maps the same way, so every figure relabels itself. And gated/pca_cluster.py hardcodes nothing about sample count: its gates re-evaluate against the sheet and flip themselves on, verified at n = 6 and n = 20. This is also what made the treatment-assignment correction above a one-line edit rather than a hunt through nine files.

Guarded, but not automatic. Three stages do not generalise: foldchange.py, replicate_check.py and qc/schema.py. They pair replicate i of control with replicate i of treated, split the workbook by its two raw sheets, and restore a specific left-row-order and column dtype after the merge — none of which has a meaning at N replicates. They read the sheet to learn which samples are which, and they assert against it, so a changed design fails loudly with “this stage is 2-channel SILAC-specific” rather than silently analysing the wrong columns. That is a tripwire, not an adaptation: those three stages must be rewritten for a new design, and the error message says so instead of pretending otherwise.

So: the inference layer scales on a config change, the ingest layer needs work, and the pipeline is built to tell you which is which at runtime. Once the replicates exist, the p-values graduate from “technical reproducibility” to “population-level biological significance” — and only then does the caveat ribbon come off. Each gated analysis switches itself on as its sample threshold is met:

  • n ≥ 6
    Real PCA & bootstrapped clustering

    Full-rank covariance; stable dendrogram topology; genuine biological-variance FDR power.

  • n ≥ 15–20
    WGCNA co-expression modules

    Correlation networks become biologically meaningful; mixOmics sPLS-DA becomes viable.

  • dozens+
    UMAP / t-SNE sample structure

    Low-dimensional embeddings stabilise; sample-level structure becomes interpretable.

  • config
    Gating needs no code change

    The dispatcher turns each method on automatically once its min_samples gate is satisfied. The three 2-channel ingest stages above still need rewriting — they will say so, loudly, on the first run.

The manual step

QIAGEN IPA is a human-run tool. The pipeline emits ipa_input.csv (all 715 regulated proteins) and a header-only ipa_input_significant.csv (0 significant, by design). Uploading to IPA and running Core / Pathway / Upstream-Regulator analyses is a licensed, Salesforce-hosted step that only a person can perform — no code can close that loop, and this report does not pretend to.

A null result, honestly kept.

The deliverable is not a list of significant proteins — there are none. It is a corrected, reproducible, self-auditing pipeline, a ranked set of hypotheses filed for the day biological replicates arrive, and a documented refusal to manufacture a story the data cannot support. At n = 2, that is the most useful thing the analysis can be.
